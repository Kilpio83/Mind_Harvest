# The Couch — Story Outline v1.0
*Main Branch Spine | Living Document — update as story evolves*

---

## Structure

This is the main branch spine — the protagonist's self-discovery arc with Bea running alongside it. No scene cards yet. Nine major beats mapping the pivots where something changes and can't be unchanged.

Patient arcs, session cards, and branching paths are developed from this spine in subsequent outline stages.

---

## The Arc

**Start:** Performing a self he didn't choose.
**End:** Living as the self he discovered — whatever it is.

The specific content of that self is determined by which patients the player invested in and how far. Two players will arrive at beat 9 having inhabited different people. The destination is the same. The person who arrives there is not.

---

## The Spine

### 1. ARRIVAL — THE INHERITED WORLD

He steps into someone else's practice. Qualification, lease, Bea — and the shadow of whoever was here before. Professional surface intact. The world is strange. He's managing it.

- Stakes loaded. Accusation shadow present but unnamed.
- Bea is warm. Everything she does is also deliberate.
- Some patients are already on the books — inherited from the previous therapist.

**Addiction loop:** Stakes established. Character to root for, something at risk, urgency supplied by the accusation's shadow and the strangeness of the inherited world.

---

### 2. THE PATIENTS ARRIVE

First sessions. Some patients knew the previous therapist. They're comfortable here in ways new patients shouldn't be. Bea is evasive about the predecessor — technically truthful, never complete. Her investment in the practice continuing is visible before its reasons are.

- Big Question loaded: *what happened here before he arrived?*
- Bea plants the first seeds toward the red room without appearing to.
- The previous therapist is referenced obliquely. Something doesn't quite add up.

**Addiction loop:** Big Question active. Sustained dopamine drip begins.

---

### 3. FIRST RESONANCE

A patient's dynamic gets through his professional distance. He follows what pulls at him. He leaves a session different from how he entered. Something in him has been found.

- First real investment. The protagonist starts being built by player choices.
- Bea notices. Her reaction is warm and something else.
- She begins assessing whether he's ready.

**Addiction loop:** First Head Fake at scene level — the session didn't go where the player expected. Rehook: what happens next session?

---

### 4. THE RED ROOM SURFACES

A patient mentions it — or implies it. Bea stops deflecting quite as smoothly. The previous therapist had a methodology. There is a second room. Some patients came here specifically for it. The protagonist faces his first real structural choice: acknowledge it or close it.

- Head Fake: this might not be transgression. It might be where the real work happens.
- For Bea: this is the moment she has been building toward since day one.
- The player's choice here begins determining which resolution branch is available.

**Addiction loop:** Major Head Fake. Everything assumed about the practice is recontextualized. Rehook: what is in that room, and what happened there?

---

### 5. ACCUMULATION — THE PROTAGONIST TRANSFORMS

Multiple patient arcs deepening simultaneously. Each investment marks him. The person walking into sessions now is different from the one who arrived. Bea's warmth deepens — genuine and strategic, both simultaneously true.

**The clue:** The protagonist searches the previous therapist's name online — professional directory, psychology listings. The last update is a specific date roughly two years ago. After that: nothing. No new practice, no retirement announcement, no social media activity. He didn't move on. He simply stopped existing. That absence loads the prediction.

- The red room, if acknowledged, becomes a real option with the right patient.
- Different patient investments produce meaningfully different protagonists at this stage.

**Addiction loop:** The Big Question sharpens. The absence of the previous therapist online is a specific, quiet, creepy rehook.

---

### 6. THE MIRROR MOMENT

The protagonist finds the previous therapist's private journal — hidden in the practice, not in the official files. The journal tracks someone going through the same arc: the same professional distance, the same sessions getting through, the same gradual self-discovery. The final entries show a person who arrived at genuine self-knowledge and then hesitated — not about the methodology, but about whether he could keep going knowing what it would cost.

The protagonist reads himself in those pages.

- This trigger is patient-independent. It works regardless of which routes the player followed.
- Professional distance collapses. The wound and the current story converge.
- The question lands: *the previous therapist found out who he was and stopped — what are you going to do?*

**Addiction loop:** The sustained Big Question finally gets its head fake — he didn't burn out. He arrived somewhere and turned back. Immediate rehook: why did he turn back, and what did Bea do about it?

---

### 7. THE RECKONING

The accusation and the current story converge. He has language for himself now. The previous therapist's full truth surfaces: he found himself and turned back. Bea's truth surfaces alongside it. She didn't let him turn back.

- The room at the back of the practice has a history now.
- Nothing can be un-known from here.
- Two paths forward: confront it or don't.

**This is the emotional peak of the main branch.** The player's most consequential choice lives here.

---

### 8. THE CHOICE

**PATH A — RED ROOM OPENED:**
The protagonist has been where the previous therapist was. He knows what Bea did and why. He decides what that means for who he's become and what he does next.

- Resolutions arrive with each deeply invested patient.
- A choice about Bea: protect her or expose her.
  - *Protect:* he absorbs what she did into his understanding of who she is and makes a decision about her from there.
  - *Expose:* consequences follow. Her fate is no longer in her own hands.

**PATH B — RED ROOM NEVER OPENED:**
Bea's goal was never reached. She stops managing from a distance.
She brings the protagonist to the red room. She kills him there — the same way, for the same reason. He hesitated. She doesn't allow hesitation.

---

### 9. RESOLUTION

**TRUE ENDING** *(Path A, deepest investment):*
He knows everything. He chooses who he is with full knowledge of where that road leads and what it cost the last person who walked it. Bea's fate shaped by his choice — protected or exposed. The practice: transformed or closed, but entirely his.

**STANDARD ENDINGS** *(Path A, partial investment):*
Self-knowledge achieved within the territory he explored. The full truth about the previous therapist glimpsed but not complete. The practice continues. Bea remains — watchful, warm, unresolved.

**DARK ENDING** *(Path B — red room never opened):*
He never opened the room. He never found out who he was. Bea's arc reaches its own conclusion. A new listing appears on the practice's directory. She is already looking for the next one.

---

## Design Notes

**On the spine's relationship to patient arcs:**
Major beats do not depend on specific patients. Beats 5, 6, and 7 are triggered by the journal and the online search — structural elements present in every playthrough. Patients shape *who* the protagonist is when he arrives at those beats, not *whether* he arrives.

**On Bea:**
Her warmth and her agenda are simultaneously true throughout. Neither cancels the other. Writers should resist making her readable as a villain before beat 7 — she should remain genuinely warm, genuinely invested, genuinely complicated until the truth surfaces.

**On the dark ending:**
The protagonist isn't punished for avoiding the red room. He's simply a guest in a story that was never about him. Bea's continuity is the point — she was here before him and she'll be here after.
