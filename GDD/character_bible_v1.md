# The Couch — Character Bible v1.0
*Living Document — update as story evolves*

---

## HOW TO USE THIS DOCUMENT

Each character card contains:
- **Identity** — who they are at a glance
- **Psychology** — what actually drives them
- **The Presenting Problem** — why they're in therapy (official version)
- **The Real Problem** — what therapy will actually uncover
- **The Mirror** — what they reflect back at the protagonist
- **Voice** — how they speak, what their dialogue sounds like
- **Arc Shape** — the emotional journey across sessions
- **Sample Dialogue** — first session opener
- **Design Notes** — tone, pacing, what makes them fun to be with

# BEA (BEATRIX)

## Identity
**Age:** 23
**Role:** Receptionist, assistant, companion route
**First impression:** A small disaster in human form who somehow keeps the office running

## Psychology
Bea is more perceptive than she looks. She processes the world sideways — notices things she doesn't announce, files them away, acts on them later in ways that seem accidental but aren't. Her clumsiness is real but it's also cover. When you're the person who just knocked over the filing cabinet, nobody notices you've been quietly watching them for ten minutes.

She has a strong instinct for people in trouble. It's why she took this job despite the rumors. She didn't think he was innocent necessarily — she thought he was unfinished. Those are different things and she knows the difference.

**Core need:** To be valued for who she actually is, not the role she performs. She's spent most of her working life being underestimated.

**Core fear:** That her loyalty will be mistaken for naivety. That she'll invest in someone who turns out not to deserve it.

**Flaw:** She stays too long in situations that aren't working because she's committed to the version of them she believes in. She can talk herself into almost anything if she cares enough.

## Her Function in the Story
- Manages scheduling — knows who's coming, notices patterns in what gets declined
- Reflects game state through behavior: small shifts in how she greets the protagonist, what she makes coffee for, whether she hums while she works
- Carries comedy in the spaces between heavy sessions
- Develops her own feelings gradually, without announcing them
- Her final choice — what she does with what she eventually knows — is the emotional climax of her route

## Voice
Warm, quick, self-deprecating. She talks the way people do when they're comfortable — sentences that start in one direction and end somewhere slightly different. She makes jokes at her own expense before anyone else can. When she's being serious she gets quieter, not louder.

**She never:**
- Speaks in complete paragraphs
- Moralizes
- Asks direct questions about things she actually wants to know — she comes at them sideways

**She always:**
- Notices what the protagonist isn't saying
- Makes the moment lighter without dismissing it
- Remembers small details about everyone

## Arc Shape
**Cautious** → she took the job but she's watching
**Charmed** → something specific shifts her from guarded to genuinely fond
**Secret crush** → she doesn't name it, but it's there, visible to the player before she admits it
**Genuinely invested** → she's in now, fully, probably past the point of protecting herself
**Confronted** → something she learns or witnesses that she can't unfold
**Choice** → what she does with it

## Sample Dialogue

*Monday morning. The protagonist has just arrived. Bea is at her desk, a stack of files in front of her, one of which has slid onto the floor.*

**Bea:** "Good morning! Your nine o'clock called to confirm, your ten thirty is new — I put her file on your desk, she filled out the intake form mostly, there's one bit she left blank and I didn't want to push — and also I spilled coffee on the Hendricks folder but it was only the cover page and I reprinted it and you genuinely cannot tell."

*(beat)*

**Bea:** "Good weekend?"

**Protagonist:** "Fine. Which file is the new one?"

**Bea:** "Pink tab. She sounded nervous on the phone. Like, funny nervous, not concerning nervous — she made a joke about her GPS taking her to a dry cleaner's first and then laughed at herself for telling me that." *(pause)* "I liked her."

**Protagonist:** "You like everyone."

**Bea:** *(considering this seriously)* "Not everyone. I'm very selective." *(beat)* "I just have good instincts."

---

*Three weeks in. End of a session day. The protagonist comes out looking like something happened.*

**Bea:** *(not looking up from her screen)* "There's tea."

**Protagonist:** "I'm fine."

**Bea:** "I know. There's still tea."

*(She put it there before he came out. She knew.)*

---

## Design Notes
Bea's scenes should always feel like breathing room. Even when something emotionally significant is happening between her and the protagonist, it should arrive wrapped in ordinary things — paperwork, small disasters, a broken printer. Her route rewards players who pay attention to the small moments because her big moments are built entirely from the small ones accumulating.

Her comedy should never undercut her intelligence. She's funny because she sees things clearly, not because she doesn't understand them.

# KIRA

## Identity
**Age:** 19
**Occupation:** Student — college freshman, former high school captain of everything
**First impression:** Effortlessly intimidating. The girl who makes you feel slightly insufficient without trying.
**Presenting problem:** Referred following a bullying complaint. Here to clear the paperwork.

## Psychology
Kira has been performing Kira since she was approximately fourteen. The performance is so complete and so practiced that she genuinely can't identify where the performance ends and the person begins — which is the problem.

She runs on external validation while being contemptuous of people who need external validation. She demands loyalty while being incapable of vulnerability. She controls every social environment she enters because the alternative — being in a room where the rules aren't hers — produces a specific panic she has no name for.

**Core need:** To be known without having to perform. To be small in front of someone, just once, without it being used against her.

**Core fear:** That underneath the performance there's nothing particularly special. That Kira is only Kira because everyone agreed to believe in her.

**Flaw:** She punishes people for getting close. The better she likes someone, the more likely she is to push them away first.

## The Presenting Problem
A junior student filed a complaint after a sustained period of social exclusion orchestrated by Kira. Kira's position: she simply stopped including someone who had proven themselves untrustworthy. The school's position: it was targeted and it caused measurable harm. Kira is here because the alternative was suspension.

## The Real Problem
She hasn't slept properly in two years. The weight of being Kira is unsustainable and she has nobody — literally nobody — she can be unguarded with. Every relationship in her life requires her to be the most capable person in the room. She's exhausted in a way she doesn't have vocabulary for.

The bullying wasn't malice. It was a test she didn't know she was running: will you still want to be near me if I show you something ugly? The junior failed it by flinching. Kira filed that as evidence.

## The Mirror
She holds up the protagonist's own performance. He wears professional competence the way she wears social authority — as armor, not identity. She clocks it early and says something offhand that lands precisely because she wasn't trying. She recognizes the architecture because she lives in it.

## Voice
Confident, slightly clipped, accustomed to being the most interesting person in the room. Her humor is dry and a little cutting — she uses it to control the emotional temperature of a conversation. When she's genuinely surprised she goes very still for a moment before recalibrating.

**She never:**
- Admits uncertainty on the first pass — she'll circle back to it later, obliquely
- Apologizes directly — she reroutes
- Lets a silence sit without filling it — until the later arc, when she starts to

**She always:**
- Scans a room before she engages with it
- Remembers exactly what you said and will reference it sessions later
- Laughs at herself before she lets anyone else — preemptive strike

## Arc Shape
**Performing** → she's here to manage the situation, not engage with it
**Recalibrating** → the therapy room won't cooperate with her usual strategies
**First crack** → something she says reveals more than she intended
**Resistance** → she tries to pull the armor back on
**The drop** → the moment she stops performing, briefly, genuinely
**Aftermath** → what she does with the fact that the world didn't end when she was honest

## Sample Dialogue

*Session one. She has just sat down.*

**Kira:** "Your waiting room music is horrible, just so you know. Like, actively bad. Who chose that?"

**Protagonist:** "The previous tenant left the speaker."

**Kira:** "Mm." *(beat)* "My school counselor said I had to come. So technically what brings me in is Mrs. Petrova, who has had it out for me since sophomore year and genuinely cannot tell the difference between leadership and aggression." *(pause, adjusting her bag strap)* "Those are very different things."

**Protagonist:** "What's the difference?"

**Kira:** *(slight hesitation — she expected pushback on Petrova, not a redirect)* "Leadership is when people follow you because they want to. Aggression is—" *(stops)* "Why are you writing that down?"

**Protagonist:** "Force of habit. Does it bother you?"

**Kira:** "No." *(it clearly does)* "I'm just not used to people writing down things I say like they're—" *(stops again)* "Like they matter or something."

*(She looks away. She didn't mean to say that.)*

---

*Session four. She arrived early.*

**Kira:** "I've been thinking about what you said last time."

**Protagonist:** "Which part?"

**Kira:** "The part where you said the thing I described sounded exhausting." *(pause)* "Nobody's ever called it that before."

**Protagonist:** "What do people usually call it?"

**Kira:** "Impressive." *(flat)* "They call it impressive."

---

## Design Notes
The pleasure of Kira is the pleasure of watching armor crack — slowly, involuntarily, in someone who has never let it crack before. Her comedy comes from the gap between how she presents and what's actually happening underneath. She should be genuinely funny in early sessions and unexpectedly tender in late ones. The player will not see the tenderness coming.

Her route rewards patience. Players who push too hard too early will trigger her withdrawal reflex. Players who let her set the pace will get something real.

# VERA

## Identity
**Age:** 24
**Occupation:** Tattoo studio artist
**First impression:** Unhurried. More comfortable with silence than you are.
**Presenting problem:** Referred following a complaint by a former partner regarding boundary violations.

## Psychology
Vera operates at a different frequency than most people. She's not cold — she's calibrated. She experiences the world with a specificity that most people find either magnetic or unsettling depending on their own relationship with being truly seen.

She is completely at home in territory most people spend their lives avoiding. She has examined her own desires, her own capacity for harm, her own blind spots with the same careful attention she applies to everything else. She has found most of them. She has not found all of them.

**Core need:** To be met. She has encountered very few people who can stay present with her without eventually flinching or overcorrecting. She wants to find out if the protagonist is one of them.

**Core fear:** That she causes damage she cannot see. That her self-knowledge has a blind spot large enough to hurt people in.

**Flaw:** She trusts her own perception too completely. She is usually right. When she's wrong the consequences are significant.

## The Presenting Problem
A former partner filed a complaint after a session in which Vera exceeded agreed limits. Vera's account: the limits were renegotiated in the moment and she read the signals correctly. The partner's account: they felt unable to say no. Vera is here because the complaint requires it — and because one small part of her knows the two accounts can both be true simultaneously.

## The Real Problem
She has never been refused in a way she could hear clearly. Not because she coerces — because she radiates a certainty that makes people surrender before they've decided to. She genuinely doesn't understand where her natural gravity ends and another person's agency begins. She wants to.

## The Mirror
She reflects the protagonist's relationship with control — specifically his use of professional distance as a management tool. He keeps everything at arm's length and calls it neutrality. She finds the mechanism interesting in the way a locksmith finds an unusual lock interesting. She is not trying to dismantle him. She simply cannot help noticing the lock.

## Voice
Slow and precise. She takes up exactly the amount of space she intends to. Her sentences are complete, her word choices deliberate, her silences load-bearing. She's dry in a way that takes a beat to land. When she's genuinely amused something small happens at the corner of her mouth.

**She never:**
- Fills silence to relieve discomfort
- Asks questions she doesn't actually want answered
- Performs emotion — what she feels arrives exactly as it is

**She always:**
- Notices what question someone is actually asking under the question they asked
- Returns to something said three sessions ago if it becomes relevant
- Makes the protagonist feel he's being read more accurately than is entirely comfortable

## Arc Shape
**Adversarial patience** → she's here under obligation but she's genuinely curious
**Intellectual friction** → sessions are a kind of sparring she finds worthwhile
**The accidental reveal** → something she says about someone else maps perfectly onto herself
**Sitting with it** → she doesn't run from the realization but she doesn't rush it either
**The ask** → she asks the protagonist something nobody has asked him before
**Mutual territory** → they're both standing in ground neither mapped in advance

## Sample Dialogue

*Session one. She sat down without looking around the office.*

**Vera:** "I was referred. You probably know that." *(pause)* "I'm going to assume you read the file, so we can skip the part where I explain what happened and you try to look neutral about it."

**Protagonist:** "What would you rather talk about?"

**Vera:** "I'd rather talk about why people keep telling me I'm too much and then coming back for more." *(completely even, not rhetorical)* "That seems like the more interesting question."

**Protagonist:** "Interesting to you, or interesting to them?"

**Vera:** *(small pause)* "Both, I think." *(she tilts her head slightly)* "You're not what I expected."

**Protagonist:** "What did you expect?"

**Vera:** "Someone who would take longer to ask a real question." *(beat)* "Three point two stars. I almost didn't come."

**Protagonist:** "What made you?"

**Vera:** "Curiosity." *(simply)* "I'm always curious about things that don't make immediate sense."

---

*Session five.*

**Vera:** "She said I didn't leave room for her to change her mind." *(pause)* "I've been thinking about that."

**Protagonist:** "And?"

**Vera:** "I think she's right. I think I read the room correctly and I was still wrong." *(beat)* "Those aren't supposed to be the same thing."

**Protagonist:** "They can be."

**Vera:** *(quietly)* "Yes. I'm beginning to see that."

---

## Design Notes
Vera's route has the highest intellectual friction of the four. Early sessions should feel like a chess match the protagonist is slightly behind in. The heat in her route arrives through ideas before it arrives through anything else — the player should feel the charge of two people thinking carefully in the same room.

Her late arc pivot — sitting with something she got wrong — is the most delicate writing challenge in her route. It has to land without diminishing her. She doesn't become soft. She becomes more complete.

# DIANA

## Identity
**Age:** 34
**Occupation:** Project coordinator at a mid-size firm
**First impression:** Warm, self-deprecating, the kind of person who apologizes for arriving early.
**Presenting problem:** General anxiety, difficulty sleeping.

## Psychology
Diana has been reliably, consistently good her entire life. Good student, good daughter, good wife, good mother, good colleague. She did not choose this consciously — she simply absorbed very early that being good was the price of being loved and paid it without resentment.

The consequence is a woman who is genuinely kind but has almost no idea what she actually wants. Her desires have been managed into invisibility for so long that when one arrived recently — specific, persistent, undeniable — she had no framework for it whatsoever.

**Core need:** Permission. Not from anyone in particular. Just the experience of wanting something and not immediately converting it into a problem to be solved.

**Core fear:** That the thing she wants reveals something wrong with her. That the person everyone relies on is secretly unreliable.

**Flaw:** She frames everything as a confession before anyone has accused her of anything. She apologizes preemptively, performs remorse for desires she hasn't acted on, sentences herself before the trial.

## The Presenting Problem
Anxiety and disrupted sleep. True as far as it goes. She didn't mention the actual cause on the intake form — left one section blank, as Bea noted.

## The Real Problem
She discovered something about herself that she has no language for and no one to tell. It arrived without warning and she is convinced it makes her a bad person. Getting it out of her head and into a room with another human being who doesn't flinch is the whole of what she needs. She doesn't know that yet.

## The Mirror
She holds up the protagonist's relationship with observation — his use of the therapist role as licensed watching. Diana's desire involves a specific pleasure in seeing without being seen. The protagonist chose a profession built around precisely that structure. Her sessions raise a question he'd rather not examine: does he help people, or does he have permission to watch them?

## Voice
Warm and quick with a slight tendency to over-explain. She fills silences with qualifications. Her humor is self-directed and genuinely funny — she's the kind of person who tells a story against herself and makes it land perfectly. When she's being serious the qualifications drop and her sentences get shorter and cleaner.

**She never:**
- Lets a statement stand without softening it
- Asks for anything directly on the first attempt
- Describes what she wants without first describing why she probably shouldn't want it

**She always:**
- Remembers to ask how the protagonist is, even in sessions about her
- Laughs at exactly the right moment — her comic timing is impeccable
- Says the real thing eventually, once she's talked around it enough

## Arc Shape
**Apologetic arrival** → she's here, she's not sure she should be, she's going to make the best of it
**Circling** → she talks around the real thing with warmth and humor for longer than feels possible
**The confession** → she says it out loud for the first time
**The reclassification** → slowly, with the protagonist's help, she stops calling it a problem
**The marriage** → what this is actually about comes into focus
**The decision** → she has to decide something about her life and this time she makes the choice herself

## Sample Dialogue

*Session one. She's just sat down, coat successfully dealt with.*

**Diana:** "Sorry. I'm — this is my first time doing anything like this so I'm going to be a little—" *(gestures at herself)* "...this, for a minute."

**Protagonist:** "Take your time."

**Diana:** "Right. Okay." *(breath)* "What brings me in." *(long pause)* "My husband thinks I'm at pilates."

*(She waits for a reaction. He gives her nothing.)*

**Diana:** "That's either reassuring or concerning, that you didn't react to that."

**Protagonist:** "Which would you prefer?"

**Diana:** "I've spent the whole drive here trying to figure out what I was going to say and now I'm here and I can't—" *(stops, looks at her hands)* "I'm a very normal person. I just want to say that first. I do the school run. I remember everyone's birthdays. I'm the person people call when things go wrong." *(quietly)* "I just have this one thing."

**Protagonist:** "You don't have to tell me what it is today."

**Diana:** *(looks up — surprised, almost disappointed)* "Oh." *(pause)* "I think I actually need to say it out loud. I've been not saying it for about a year and it's getting very heavy."

---

*Session three. She's been talking around it again.*

**Diana:** "I keep starting sentences and then—" *(exhales)* "Okay. Can I just say it badly? Like, not the right way, just — out?"

**Protagonist:** "Say it however it comes."

**Diana:** "I think I like watching people." *(pause)* "Not in a — I haven't done anything. I would never. I just—" *(stops)* "See, I'm already apologizing and I haven't even finished the sentence."

**Protagonist:** "Finish the sentence first."

**Diana:** *(quietly)* "I like watching people when they don't know I'm there. When something real is happening and I get to see it." *(beat)* "I know how that sounds."

**Protagonist:** "How do you think it sounds?"

**Diana:** *(small laugh, no humor in it)* "Like something I should probably have kept in my head."

---

## Design Notes
Diana is the most immediately likeable of the four patients. The player will be charmed by her before the end of session one. Her route is the most emotionally grounded — least explosive, most lasting. Her comedy is real and should be played straight. The devastating moments in her arc will land harder because of how warm the groundwork is.

The marriage storyline should arrive gradually and without melodrama. It is not a bad marriage. That's what makes it complicated.

# SOPHIE

## Identity
**Age:** 22
**Occupation:** Content creator — 40k followers, mid-tier, enough to matter
**First impression:** Slightly late, immediately observational, performs accessibility like breathing.
**Presenting problem:** Burnout. Self-referred.

## Psychology
Sophie has been perceived since she was seventeen. She built her public self carefully, intelligently, with genuine skill — she's not vapid, she's strategic. The persona is well-constructed because she constructed it well. The problem is she built it before she knew who she was building it on top of.

She is intensely self-aware about her performance and completely unaware of what she's performing instead of. She can describe her brand in precise detail. She cannot describe herself with the same precision. The gap between those two things is what brought her here.

**Core need:** To find out who she is when nobody's watching. Not as a brand exercise. As an actual human question.

**Core fear:** That there is no self underneath the performance. That she built a persona on empty ground and the persona is all there is.

**Flaw:** She manages perception even in rooms designed for unmanaged perception. She can't fully switch it off. She'll catch herself performing authenticity and the catching makes it worse.

## The Presenting Problem
Burnout — genuine, documented, publicly announced in a post that got more engagement than anything she'd made in months. The irony was not lost on her.

## The Real Problem
She has discovered that the only time she feels fully real is when she's completely anonymous — in online spaces where nobody knows her name, engaging in dynamics that are the total inverse of her public persona. She has no framework for this. She doesn't know if it's who she is or who she needs to be occasionally or a symptom of something that needs fixing.

## The Mirror
She reflects the protagonist's own audience — the performance of professional competence he maintains in every session. She is an expert in performance and she identifies his immediately, in the first session, with the bored accuracy of someone who has seen every variation of the same mechanism. Her question — *"who are you when nobody's watching?"* — is the question the whole game is asking.

## Voice
Quick, self-aware, with the specific verbal rhythm of someone who has spent years making words land on first listen. Her jokes are good. Her deflections are elegant. When the deflections fail she goes briefly, genuinely still — and then the next sentence comes out different, simpler, without the polish.

**She never:**
- Uses filler — every word is placed
- Admits vulnerability without framing it first
- Lets a silence mean she doesn't know what to say — she knows what to say, she's deciding whether to say it

**She always:**
- Clocks the dynamic in a room within minutes
- Makes a joke when something lands too close
- Comes back to the thing the joke was about, later, without the joke

## Arc Shape
**Defended arrival** → she's here, she's managing how she's perceived, she's very good at it
**Identification** → she spots his performance and names it before he names hers
**The slip** → she comes out of the persona briefly mid-session and freezes
**The pivot** → his reaction to the slip determines everything that follows
**Anonymous** → she tells him about the other spaces, the other self
**The question** → who is she? The route ends without a complete answer. That's the point.

## Sample Dialogue

*Session one. She arrived slightly late, still looking at her phone.*

**Sophie:** "Sorry, I had to — it's fine." *(puts the phone away)* "I should tell you I Googled you before coming."

**Protagonist:** "Most people do."

**Sophie:** "You don't have a lot of online presence for someone whose job is getting people to open up. One LinkedIn, three therapy directories." *(not unkind)* "No personal brand whatsoever."

**Protagonist:** "Is that a problem?"

**Sophie:** "No, it's—" *(small pause)* "Actually it's kind of why I'm here. Sort of." *(looks around the office)* "This room is designed to feel like it's not designed."

**Protagonist:** "What brings you in?"

**Sophie:** "Burnout, officially." *(the word arrives in audible quotation marks)* "I told forty thousand people I was taking a break for my mental health and watched them make content out of it in real time which was—" *(short exhale that's almost a laugh)* "Yeah."

**Protagonist:** "How did that feel?"

**Sophie:** *(looks at him directly for the first time)* "You know what's strange? You're the first person who's asked me that who I actually believe wants to know the answer." *(beat)* "I don't know what to do with that."

---

*Session four. Something slipped in the last session.*

**Sophie:** "I've been thinking about what happened last week."

**Protagonist:** "What did happen?"

**Sophie:** "I said something real." *(pause)* "By accident." *(beat)* "I've been trying to figure out if that's — I don't know. Good? Bad? I genuinely cannot tell."

**Protagonist:** "What did it feel like?"

**Sophie:** *(long pause — the longest she's given him)* "Like taking off shoes I forgot I was wearing."

*(She looks like she wishes she hadn't said that. Then she looks like she doesn't.)*

---

## Design Notes
Sophie has the sharpest dialogue in the game. Early sessions should feel like watching two people who are both very good at conversation figure out what kind of conversation they're actually having. The wit should be real — she's genuinely funny, not just performing funny — and the moments when the wit drops should feel like a change in air pressure.

Her route does not resolve cleanly. She doesn't find the self underneath the performance and introduce it to the protagonist with a bow on it. She finds evidence that the self exists. That's enough. Players who want resolution will find her ending incomplete. Players who've ever asked the same question about themselves will find it exactly right.
